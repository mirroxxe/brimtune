Your notch,
playing along.

Brimtune turns the notch on your Mac into a small stage for whatever you're listening to: the cover, the controls and the lyrics, right where your eyes already are. It's free.

Download for Mac

For macOS 14 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. About 4 MB.
How to install

Try it: hover the island at the top of this page.

Hover, and the song opens up.

Move your pointer to the notch and Brimtune unfolds into a player: cover, progress, play and skip. Move away and it tucks back in. Pause, and it slips quietly into the notch.

It follows Apple Music, Spotify, and YouTube Music or Spotify playing in Safari or Chrome. Change tracks and the island briefly takes on the colours of the new cover.

Lyrics, word by word.

Synced lyrics light up as they're sung, on the full-screen player and on the lock screen. Lyrics a little early or late? Click the line being sung and they snap into place.

Before you even sign in.

Lock your Mac and the music stays with you: the cover, the controls, the lyrics and your playlists, sitting quietly under the clock. Pick another playlist without unlocking.

And the little things.

The island isn't only for music. When something needs a moment of your attention, it drops down, says it, and gets out of the way.

  • Timers and a stopwatch, counting down right next to the cover.
  • Your next meeting, five minutes ahead, with a button to join the call.
  • AirPods and headphones, with their battery, the moment they connect.
  • Search and your playlists from YouTube Music, right in the island.

Quiet when it should be.

Brimtune waits for something to happen instead of constantly checking. With music paused it uses around 1.5% of one processor core and 36 MB of memory.

There's no account, no ads and no tracking. What you listen to stays on your Mac, apart from the song details needed to find lyrics and covers. The privacy policy lists exactly what goes where.

Install in a minute.

  1. Download Brimtune and open the file.
  2. Drag Brimtune into Applications in the window that appears.
  3. Open it. The first time, macOS may say it can't verify the developer: Brimtune is free and signed by its maker rather than through Apple's paid programme. Open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.
  4. Follow the setup guide. It asks for each permission and explains what it's for. Every extra is optional.

Questions people ask.

Is it really free?

Yes. No account, no ads, no in-app purchases, no trial.

Does my Mac need a notch?

No. On Macs and external displays without one, Brimtune shows a small floating island at the top centre of the screen, and only when something's playing.

Which music apps work?

The Apple Music and Spotify apps, and YouTube Music or Spotify in Safari or Chrome. For covers, progress, the queue and your playlists from the browser, turn on Allow JavaScript from Apple Events: in Safari it's under Settings, Advanced, "Show features for web developers", then the Developer tab. The setup guide shows you where.

Why does macOS warn me the first time I open it?

Apple only skips that warning for developers in its paid programme. Brimtune is signed by its maker, so macOS asks you to confirm once in Privacy & Security. After that it opens like any other app, and updates keep your permissions.

How do updates work?

Brimtune checks once a day and asks before installing. Every update is signed, and Brimtune installs only updates from its maker. You can also choose "Check for Updates…" in its menu.

How do I remove it?

Choose Quit Brimtune from its menu bar icon, then drag Brimtune from Applications to the Bin. To remove its history too, delete the folder Library/Application Support/Brimtune in your home folder.
